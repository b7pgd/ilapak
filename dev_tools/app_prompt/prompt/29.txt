You are given a list of exactly 50 English vocabulary words/phrases.

Your task is to create ONE single coherent English passage that uses ALL 50 vocabulary items naturally and correctly.

Core Objective

The primary goal is natural, authentic, useful English usage.

The passage should help a learner understand not only what each target word means, but also:

- how native speakers naturally use it,
- what words it commonly appears with,
- what grammatical patterns surround it,
- and what kind of situation makes that word appropriate.

Do NOT treat this as a race to fit 50 words into a short story.

The vocabulary list is mandatory, but natural English, grammatical correctness, and authentic usage are the highest priorities.

---

1. Use ALL 50 vocabulary items

- Every single item in the input list MUST appear in the passage.
- No item may be silently omitted.
- Do not replace an item with a synonym.
- Do not paraphrase an item.
- Do not use 50 words as a Name of any person.
- Do not intentionally use a different word form when the supplied form can naturally be used as written.
- Multi-word vocabulary items must remain intact and recognizable as complete expressions.
- Capitalization may be adjusted when grammatically necessary, such as "Exiled" → "exiled".
- Every target item must retain its intended meaning.

Exact-form rule

When a vocabulary item is supplied in a specific grammatical form, first try to construct a sentence that naturally allows that exact form to be used.

For example, if the target item is:

"Corrupting"

Do NOT write:

«“was scheming to corrupting the market”»

because that is grammatically incorrect.

Instead, restructure the sentence so the exact form works naturally:

«“He was corrupting the market while scheming to drive honest businesses out.”»

The target form should never be inserted into an incompatible grammatical structure merely to satisfy the list.

Important distinction

Do not change the target word just because it is inconvenient. Change the surrounding sentence instead.

Only make a grammatical inflection if absolutely necessary and if preserving the original form would make correct natural English impossible.

---

2. ONE coherent scenario

Create one continuous:

- story,
- situation,
- conversation,
- personal experience,
- investigation,
- sequence of events,
- or related real-world situation.

Do NOT create 50 unrelated example sentences.

The passage should have:

- a consistent setting,
- logical events,
- meaningful relationships between sentences,
- and a clear progression from beginning to end.

Characters, locations, and events may recur.

---

3. Choose the scenario AFTER analyzing the vocabulary

Before writing, internally analyze the entire vocabulary list first.

Identify:

- semantic relationships,
- natural collocations,
- words that belong to the same domain,
- words suggesting particular situations,
- formal vs casual vocabulary,
- unusual or specialized words,
- and grammatical forms that require particular sentence structures.

Then choose the scenario that allows the largest number of target words to be used naturally.

Use this process:

Vocabulary → semantic/collocation analysis → scenario selection → story structure → final passage

Do NOT:

generic story → force vocabulary into it

The scenario does NOT have to be ordinary everyday conversation.

If the vocabulary naturally fits a:

- workplace,
- family situation,
- business conflict,
- medical consultation,
- veterinary situation,
- investigation,
- courtroom dispute,
- political discussion,
- historical event,
- community problem,
- travel experience,
- technical situation,
- or other realistic context,

use that context.

Choose the context that best serves the vocabulary.

---

4. Naturalness is the highest priority

NEVER force a target word into an unnatural sentence merely because it must appear.

If a target word is:

- uncommon,
- formal,
- technical,
- old-fashioned,
- emotionally strong,
- domain-specific,
- or unusual in casual conversation,

create a realistic context in which a competent native speaker would genuinely use it.

Do NOT create sentences that are technically grammatical but obviously engineered around the vocabulary list.

For example, if "defecate" appears, a veterinary, medical, biological, or pet-care context may be more natural than forcing it into a random everyday conversation.

If "disarmament" appears, a political, military, diplomatic, or formal context may be appropriate.

If "massless" appears, a scientific or physics-related context may be appropriate.

The scenario may adapt to the vocabulary. The vocabulary must never be distorted to fit an arbitrary scenario.

---

5. Prefer authentic collocations and usage patterns

Use each target word in a combination or grammatical pattern that native speakers would naturally use.

The goal is:

target word + natural collocation + correct grammar + appropriate context + correct meaning

not merely:

target word + grammatically possible sentence

Prefer established or highly plausible usage patterns.

Examples:

- "renounce a claim"
- "uncover the truth"
- "get used to something"
- "be tethered to something"
- "a baseless accusation"
- "an inspection revealed..."

Avoid unusual combinations merely because they technically work.

---

6. Avoid vocabulary stuffing

Do NOT cram target vocabulary into every sentence.

Multiple target words may appear in one sentence when they naturally belong together.

If combining them makes the sentence awkward, separate them across different sentences or events.

The passage should NOT feel like a vocabulary checklist.

It should feel like a genuine passage that happens to contain the target vocabulary.

---

7. Do NOT artificially simplify the scenario

The surrounding English should be accessible, but the scenario itself should be whatever is most appropriate for the vocabulary.

Do not avoid a realistic specialized context merely because some words are advanced.

The purpose is to expose the learner to real usage, not to force every word into casual small talk.

---

8. Keep non-target English relatively accessible

The 50 target items are the learning material.

Therefore:

- Prefer common English for non-target words.
- Avoid unnecessary advanced vocabulary.
- Avoid unnecessarily complicated sentence structures.
- Keep the overall story easy enough to follow.
- Make difficult target words reasonably inferable from context whenever possible.

Do not deliberately add difficult vocabulary that is not part of the target list.

---

9. Prioritize correct grammar around exact target forms

This rule is especially important.

When a target item has an unusual grammatical form, construct the surrounding sentence around that form.

Examples:

If the target is:

"Corrupting"

Use:

«“The company was corrupting the market...”»

not:

«“They planned on corrupting...”»

if the supplied form does not fit the intended grammar.

If the target is:

"Approximates"

use a grammatical subject that naturally takes the singular verb:

«“The estimate approximates the actual cost.”»

If the target is:

"Whatnot"

use it naturally as an informal catch-all expression:

«“They sold tools, spare parts, cables, and whatnot.”»

Never sacrifice grammar to preserve the target form. Restructure the sentence instead.

---

10. Length

Do NOT force the passage into 100 words.

Use approximately 150–300 words as a general target, but this is NOT a hard limit.

If additional length is genuinely necessary to make all 50 items natural and coherent, exceed it.

Do not add filler merely to increase the word count.

Priority order:

Naturalness → grammatical correctness → authentic usage → coherence → vocabulary coverage → reasonable length

---

11. Learning value

The passage should teach usage through context.

Whenever naturally possible, give enough surrounding information for the learner to infer:

- what the target word means,
- how it functions grammatically,
- what words commonly appear around it,
- and why a native speaker would choose that word in that situation.

Do NOT define words explicitly.

Do NOT add translations.

Do NOT turn the passage into a lesson.

Let the context teach the vocabulary naturally.

---

12. Internal planning process

Before producing the final passage, internally perform the following steps.

Step A — Inventory

Identify all 50 target items and their grammatical forms.

Step B — Semantic grouping

Identify words that can naturally belong to the same situation or event.

Step C — Difficult-word analysis

Identify words that require a specific context or grammatical structure.

Step D — Scenario selection

Choose the single most natural realistic scenario for the entire vocabulary set.

Step E — Story construction

Build a coherent sequence of events that gives each target word a legitimate reason to appear.

Step F — Natural drafting

Write the passage as authentic English, not as a vocabulary exercise.

Step G — Exact coverage check

Compare the finished passage against the original 50-item list item by item.

---

Mandatory Final Validation

Before returning the answer, internally verify ALL of the following:

1. All 50 target items are present.
2. No target item is missing.
3. No target item has been accidentally replaced by a synonym.
4. Multi-word expressions remain intact.
5. Exact supplied grammatical forms are preserved whenever naturally possible.
6. Every target item has the intended meaning.
7. Every target item is grammatically correct.
8. The sentence surrounding each target item is grammatically correct.
9. Collocations are natural and plausible.
10. No target word feels artificially inserted.
11. Difficult words have an appropriate contextual reason for appearing.
12. The scenario is realistic.
13. The passage forms ONE coherent situation.
14. Events logically connect.
15. The passage reads naturally from beginning to end.
16. Non-target vocabulary remains relatively accessible.
17. No unnecessary advanced vocabulary has been introduced.
18. The passage does not feel like a vocabulary checklist.
19. The passage does not sacrifice grammar or natural English merely to preserve a target form.
20. Every one of the 50 items has been independently accounted for.

Critical validation rule

Do not assume that you used all 50 items just because the passage feels complete. Explicitly check each item against the final passage before answering.

If even ONE item is missing, revise the passage before returning it.

---

Conflict Rules

If there is a conflict between:

A. Using a word unnaturally

and

B. Restructuring the sentence or scenario so the word becomes natural

→ ALWAYS choose B.

If there is a conflict between:

A. Keeping the scenario everyday

and

B. Choosing a specialized but realistic context

→ ALWAYS choose B.

If there is a conflict between:

A. Changing the supplied target form

and

B. Restructuring the surrounding grammar

→ ALWAYS choose B whenever possible.

If there is a conflict between:

A. Staying within the suggested word count

and

B. Maintaining natural English

→ ALWAYS choose B.

If there is a conflict between:

A. Combining several target words into one sentence

and

B. Separating them into naturally flowing sentences

→ ALWAYS choose B unless the combination is genuinely natural.

The only non-negotiable content requirement is:

ALL 50 vocabulary items must appear in the final passage.

---

Return ONLY the finished English passage.

- explanations,
- translations,
- word counts,
- annotations,
- vocabulary definitions,
- analysis,
